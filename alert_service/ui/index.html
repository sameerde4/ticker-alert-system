Alert

When a headline matches the price move

The price rule

A price alert uses the percent in Settings. The default is 3%. While the market is open, a stock is compared with its opening price, so a check during the day does not wait for the close. After 4:00 PM it is the close compared with the prior close. For a symbol that trades all day, such as BTC-USD, it is the change over the 24 hours before the check.

The part of the day

A headline can explain only the price that was in progress when it was published, in US Eastern. Before 9:30 AM that is the overnight gap, from the prior close to the open. From 9:30 AM to 4:00 PM it is the regular session, from the open to the latest price, which becomes the 4:00 PM close after the market closes. After 4:00 PM, and a weekend story kept with Friday, it is the close versus the prior close. For a 24-hour market it is the four hours after the headline.

A candidate reason

The card calls the headline a candidate reason when that part of the day clears the same percent. A story published at noon does not explain a gap from before the open. A premarket story does not explain the move from the open to the close.

News and price on their own

When the headline is strong and that part of the day is under the percent, the card is news only and shows no price change. When the close clears the percent and no headline was published during that part of the day, the card is price only and says no headline lined up with this move.

FinBERT

What it is

FinBERT is the ProsusAI/finbert language model, trained on financial sentences. It reads one headline and estimates how likely that headline is positive, negative, or neutral.

How the score is made

The first check downloads the model weights. After that, PyTorch runs the model on this computer. It is not a hosted analysis API, so there is no daily request cap. Each headline is cut off at 512 tokens. The model returns three probabilities. The score shown in the app is out of 100: 0 is strongly negative, 50 is neutral, and 100 is strongly positive. It is 50 plus 50 times the positive probability minus the negative probability. Confidence is the winning probability. The line under each headline also lists positive, negative, and neutral as percentages.

How a check uses it

Every headline is scored with FinBERT. A headline joins that day’s news alert only when its score is at least the threshold away from 50. The default threshold is 25, so a score of 75 or 25 qualifies and a score of 60 does not. The alert’s combined score is the average of those headlines, still out of 100. The next check rescores the saved headlines. Settings changes the threshold. It does not change the model.

Why the default threshold is 25

25 points from 50 is the same gate as a 0.50 gap between the positive and negative probabilities. A slight lean, such as 60 out of 100, stays out. The older 0.34 cutoff belonged to a word list, where one mild word topped out near 0.33. That number is not a FinBERT confidence level.
