Historical prices (lake)

The lake under data/lake/raw/prices/daily/ trains Isolation Forest. On first launch the app lands about one year of S&P prices and trains automatically; it also refreshes and retrains on a daily schedule. The buttons below are manual overrides. Land replaces the lake. Refresh checks each symbol’s latest lake date against the last completed session (before 4:00 PM Eastern that is still yesterday; after the close it is today). If everything is already through that session it skips Yahoo and retrain — live Kafka minute bars do not write the lake. Otherwise it only pulls what’s missing (lookback capped at ~2 weeks) and then retrains. Neither job changes saved alerts.

Alert

Price gates and when news matches

Two price gates

A price-led day opens when either gate fires on Live or Watchlist Scan today. (1) Price move threshold in Settings — default 3%: open→last while the market is open, or close vs prior close after the close. (2) Isolation Forest from the lake registry — the same four features used in training (gap, session, close, volume z-score). Forest can fire even when the move is under the threshold. What happened tags the case: percent only, percent + forest, forest only, or neither gate.

The part of the day

A headline can explain only the price that was in progress when it was published, in US Eastern. Before 9:30 AM that is the overnight gap, from the prior close to the open. From 9:30 AM to 4:00 PM it is the regular session, from the open to the latest price, which becomes the 4:00 PM close after the market closes. After 4:00 PM, and a weekend story kept with Friday, it is the close versus the prior close.

A candidate reason

The card calls the headline a candidate reason when that part of the day clears the same price move threshold. A story published at noon does not explain a gap from before the open. A premarket story does not explain the move from the open to the close.

News and price on their own

When the headline is strong and that part of the day is under the threshold (and forest did not fire), the card is news only. When price clears a gate and the same-day headline check finds that the news does not explain that slice, the card is price only — with that grade on the card.

FinBERT

What it is

FinBERT is the ProsusAI/finbert language model, trained on financial sentences. It reads one headline and estimates how likely that headline is positive, negative, or neutral.

How the score is made

The first check downloads the model weights. After that, PyTorch runs the model on this computer. It is not a hosted analysis API, so there is no daily request cap. Each headline is cut off at 512 tokens. The model returns three probabilities. The score shown in the app is out of 100: 0 is strongly negative, 50 is neutral, and 100 is strongly positive. It is 50 plus 50 times the positive probability minus the negative probability. Confidence is the winning probability. The line under each headline also lists positive, negative, and neutral as percentages.

How a check uses it

Every headline is scored with FinBERT. A headline joins that day’s news alert only when its score is at least the FinBERT sentiment threshold away from 50 (Settings). The default is 25, so a score of 75 or 25 qualifies and a score of 60 does not. The alert’s combined score is the average of those headlines. Isolation Forest is the separate price/volume model trained on the lake — it does not score headlines.

Why the default threshold is 25

25 points from 50 is the same gate as a 0.50 gap between the positive and negative probabilities. A slight lean, such as 60 out of 100, stays out. The older 0.34 cutoff belonged to a word list, where one mild word topped out near 0.33. That number is not a FinBERT confidence level.
